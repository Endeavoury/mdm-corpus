Codelits            BVN mentions that the topic related to codelists was presented and
                    subsequently discussed during the last GeoDCAT-AP webinar on 14th
Slides 10 - 37      May 2024. However, it has not yet been addressed within the broader
                    DCAT-AP community. The objective is to reach a consensus on a
Speaker: Bert Van   concrete proposal.
Nuffelen
                    The primary issue discussed in this section is the necessity to establish
                    a common interpretation for the use of codelists in DCAT-AP.
                    Specifically, the webinar addressed the interpretation of qualifications
                    such as 'must,' 'is recommended,' and 'may' for codelists. The objective
                    is to provide clear semantics for these qualifiers to ensure consistent
                    implementation and validation across different profiles.

                    Multiple systems scenario
                    A scenario was presented to illustrate the challenges of using codelists
                    across multiple systems:

                       •   Scenario: A publisher publishes successfully for System 1 but
                           fails for System 2 due to different codelist requirements.
                       •   Solution: The webinar proposed the need for combined
                           guidelines for publishers to ensure consistent validation across
                           different systems

                    Multiple systems scenario issue arises due to two interpretations for the
                    mandatory codelist. BVN explained the two interpretations for
                    mandatory codelists:
                       • Interpretation 1: The value space is closed under the codelist,
                            meaning only values from the codelist are allowed.
                                o Strict validation: If a property has a value outside the
                                   codelist, it results in a validation violation.
                                o Application: Conformant to the way literal value spaces
                                   are expressed, making it suitable for programming
                                   languages and most software systems.
                                o Validation: Existing SHACL shapes can be used as-is,
                                   making validation simple and direct. Violations drive the
                                   feedback, ensuring strict adherence to the codelist.
                       • Interpretation 2: At least one value from the codelist is required,
                            allowing for additional values from other codelists
                                o Application: where there is a need for aggregation at the
                                   level of properties, allowing for more flexibility.
                                o Validation: Existing SHACL shapes cannot be used as-is
                                   and require a filtering process as part of the processing
                                   and conformance building. This filtering process is non-
                                   trivial to standardise and implement, making poor
                                   usages harder to detect.
                                        ▪ Matching SHACL shapes can be designed
The choice between these two interpretations depends on the desired
level of strictness and flexibility in the implementation of codelists.
Cross-profile assessment:
    • Interpretation 1: More strict, requiring coordination among
        profiles to avoid conflicts. For example, if DCAT-AP uses
        Interpretation 2 and GeoDCAT-AP uses Interpretation 1, the
        usage in DCAT-AP would also need to adhere to Interpretation
        1.
    • Interpretation 2: Allows for more flexibility but still requires
        coordination to ensure consistent implementation across
        profiles.

Many participants believe that Interpretation 1 is harmful to
interoperability and violates the open world assumption due to its
limitation of a property to just one codelist. This view is widely
supported, with many participants preferring Interpretation 2, as
reflected in the general sentiment of the chat.

Issue #408:
The codelists “dct:type (Agent Type)” and “dct:type (licence type)” are
currently interpreted as a ‘must” in the qualification table. BVN raised
concerns that the existing code lists for agents and licenses may not be
comprehensive enough, or sufficiently centralised and harmonised to
cover all necessary cases.
SEMIC proposition:
Change the qualification of the codelists “dct:type (Agent Type)” and
“dct:type (licence type)” from "must" to "recommended". It is still
suggested to use these code lists, but not on a mandatory basis,
allowing for more flexibility in their application.




Resolution:
LR agreed on this proposal, as he raised the issue himself earlier.
There were no objections from the participants. BVN emphasised that
any comments should be directed to the relevant GitHub issue.

Issue #409:
BVN highlights the driving issue regarding the use of codelists
“dcat:theme”. There are two formulations for the codelist “dcat:theme”:
the individual usage note formulates the usage of "at least one" and the
codelist section formulates the usage of "must," which corresponds to a
stricter interpretation.
The conflict affects the consistency and interoperability of “dcat:theme”
and its associated codelists across different profiles. In practice many
data portals have aligned with the EU NAL dataset theme vocabulary
by extending it or mapping to it (e.g. INSPIRE)
SEMIC proposition:
The goal is to resolve this conflict and provide clear guidance on which
interpretation to use. Three proposals are presented by BVN:

   •   Proposition A - MUST:
       All other DCAT-AP profiles that would like to share their theme
       must introduce a subproperty of dcat:theme. dcat:theme
       stays paired exclusively with the EU NAL Dataset theme
       Vocabulary. Conflict stays with profiles beyond EU boundaries.
       Bug fix release of the specification

   •   Proposition B – AT LEAST 1:
       All data Catalogues must adapt their uniqueness expectation in
       the theme property and implement a filter based on the theme
       they like to show in the UI. The used codelists MUST be
       published as proper Linked Data in the public space. To
       facilitate every portal to make easy decisions.
       Bug fix release of the specification.

   •   Proposition C – Conflict free resolution:
       Replace the use of dcat:theme in DCAT-AP with a subproperty
       of dcat:theme. The new property r5r:theme stays paired
       exclusively with the EU NAL Dataset theme Vocabulary.
       Minor release of the specification.

Resolution:
Priyanka mentioned that Proposition A is preferred because it allows for
easier combination with other domains. She stated "A is preferred,
otherwise you can't combine with other domains". When asked to
elaborate, she explained that from a harvester's point of view, having a
controlled vocabulary makes it easier to filter on certain domains.
Without a controlled vocabulary, one would first have to figure out what
all keywords relate to a specific domain, such as health. MP responded
by sharing their experience with the Swedish data portal. They
confirmed that this is a real problem and explained that they typically
use faceting based on themes.

SVD addresses the concern that if the controlled vocabulary is limited
to just one, it will hinder the adoption of other taxonomies. SVD agrees
with MP's earlier point about using controlled vocabularies. SVD
highlights the flexibility in expressing domain vocabularies and
performing faceting and drill-down operations without being explicit, but
notes that this introduces a runtime dependency. This flexibility allows
for extending the system without writing new code and supporting
multiple ways of faceting in a catalogue. SVD also mentions that fixing
the system to one controlled vocabulary and requiring something not in
DCAT seems counterintuitive. They suggest that it might be better to
use the construct present in DCAT as intended and create a sub-
property if something specific is needed, while expressing his
preference for proposition B.
BVN remarks regarding proposition B it's crucial to publish and use
code lists according to linked data principles. Failing to do so could lead
to potential chaos. JK summarises from the chat that votes for B are
slightly more favoured than for A. Therefore, further discussion on this
issue will be moved to the GitHub.

GeoDCAT-AP          The relaxation is regarding the cardinality of the property dct:rights.
alignment           BVN explains that historically rights were limited to one document
                    listing all rights. However, in GeoDCAT-AP, metadata often comes from
Slides 38 - 42      translation of ISO to DCAT-AP, resulting in individual statements split
                    into multiple lines and descriptions. This means there are multiple rights
Speaker: Bert Van   documents instead of just one. In DCAT-AP dct:rights is used for
Nuffelen            Catalogue and Distribution, with the current cardinality 0..1, in
                    GeoDCAT-AP, the cardinality was relaxed to 0..*. In DCAT-AP HVD,
                    dct:rights with cardinality 0..* is used also for Data Services.
                    SEMIC Proposition:
                    Given this history, it was proposed to increase cardinality. Relax
                    cardinality for dct:rights to 0..* everywhere in DCAT-AP.
                    Resolution
                    There were no objections, and the relaxation is accepted.

                    Issue #384:
                    BVN explained the conflicting cardinality of the spatial resolution with
                    GeoDCAT-AP. There was a limitation in DCAT-AP where only one
                    spatial resolution was available at the data set level. The community
                    pointed out that spatial resolution for data sets should describe the
                    resolutions in the original data, which might be captured in different
                    resolutions.
                    SEMIC proposition:
                    Relax the DCAT-AP 3.0 cardinality of spatial resolution for Dataset to
                    0..*.
                    Resolution:
                    There were no objections, and the relaxation has been accepted.

                    Issue #175 & #265; GeoDCAT-AP #68:
                    BVN emphasised the need for multiple representations of location
                    information (WKT, GML, GeoJSON) due to varying formats.
                    Current SHACL constraints allow only one instance of locn:geometry,
                    dcat:bbox, and dcat:centroid. However, GeoDCAT-AP permits multiple
                    instances, representing one entity, described using different geometry
                    encodings, e.g. WKT, GML and GeoJSON, leading to SHACL
                    validation errors.
                    SEMIC proposition:
                    The current bounding box cardinality is 1, but multiple representations
                    are needed, similar to multilingual texts, by lifting the cardinalities to
                    0..*.
                    Resolution:
                    MP suggested choosing a single format (WKT or GML) since GeoJSON
                    has only one coordinate reference system. The issue of duplicate
                    metadata was raised, and it was proposed that the community should
                    harmonise on one format. JK highlighted that there was no consensus
                    observed during the GeoDCAT-AP discussions on WKT or GML due to
                    their different expressive powers. BVN agreed to take a note, as no
                    immediate decision could be made, and community alignment was
                    needed. JE agreed with MP's comment and additionally noted that in
                    the final GeoDCAT-AP document, the metadata should be presented in
                    a single, selected format.

                    Issue #348:
                    BVN mentioned the issue regarding identifying data set series in the
                    catalog, as there is no direct property in DCAT for this purpose. How to
                    include a dcat:DatasetSeries directly into a dcat:Catalog? In DCAT-AP
                    3.0.0, dcat:DatasetSeries is not a subclass of dcat:Dataset but of
                    dcat:Resource, creating ambiguity in how to properly include it in a
                    catalog.
                    SEMIC proposition:
                        • No support, wait for W3C (no release)
                        • Introduce a separate r5r:datasetSeries property (equivalent to
                            dcat:Dataset or dcat:DataService) (minor release)
                        • Replace the use of dcat:Dataset and dcat:DataService with
                            dcat:Resource and then it is up to the system to address the
                            expectations. (minor release)
                    Resolution:
                    MP questioned the necessity of distinguishing between data sets and
                    data set series, suggesting that the type property might complicate
                    matters, MP proposed the usage of dcat:Dataset. The community
                    needs to decide on the best approach to address this issue, with a call
                    for participation on the GitHub issue to gather more viewpoints and
                    collaboratively come to a concrete proposition for the autumn 2025
                    webinar.

                    Issue #402:
                    BVN highlighted the DCAT-AP use of SPDX for checksums to
                    determine the status and quality of distributions. The release of SPDX
                    3.0 introduced significant changes from SPDX 2.0, including new URIs
                    and a different structure. This change impacts DCAT-AP users who rely
on SPDX 2.0 for checksum representation, as they would need to
adapt to the new version without gaining additional benefits.
SEMIC proposition:
    • Option A: replace SPDX 2.3 with SPDX 3.0 (minor release)
    • Option B: do not replace SPDX 2.3 but add usage note (bug fix
        release)
Resolution:
MC suggested to support both versions, allowing users to choose
based on their system dependencies. However, this approach might
complicate implementation for portal users. MP emphasised the need
for a principle to handle dependencies on external vocabularies and the
potential impact of such changes on the community. MP also
mentioned the possible usage of proxy vocabularies in case there isn’t
commitment from the external vocabulary. The community needs to
decide on the best approach to address this issue, with a call for
participation on the GitHub issue.

Issue #386:
The current limitation in DCAT-AP, which allows only one publisher for

SEMIC proposition:
The proposal is the usage of foaf:Group instead if foaf:Agent for the

Resolution:
There were no comments provided by the participants.

Issue #404:
BVN highlighted the issue that DCAT-AP provides no option for data
providers to make annotations regarding the intended target audience
of a dataset.
SEMIC proposition:
Target audience is a vague concept. Objective is very portal usage
dependent. Therefore, sharing it might be not informative.
Proposal not to adopt this.
Resolution:
SS raised this issue because it relevance for more specific variants of
DCAT-AP, such as in the health sector, where it might be important to
distinguish between datasets for scientists and politicians. However, it
was suggested that this should be handled in sector-specific DCAT-AP
variants rather than in the central DCAT-AP. MG commented that it is
not a good property to include in DCAT-AP due to the higher risk of
misinterpretation. The general consensus was to not adopt the
proposal.
